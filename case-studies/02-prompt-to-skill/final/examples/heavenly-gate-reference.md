# Heavenly Gate Reference

This is the user's original long-running successful prompt. Use it as a calibration case for spatial layering, restrained grandeur, architectural specificity, and observed rather than staged composition.

Do not treat its gate, dawn light, left-side ivory banner, tiny ascending immortals, cloud placement, or exact palette as permanent requirements for every scene. The permanent requirements are defined in `SKILL.md`.

## Original prompt

> A 21:9 cinematic live-action xianxia fantasy still, viewed from behind a large hanging silk banner within the Chinese Heavenly Court. The camera is concealed at the ceremonial edge of a high terrace, physically close to the fabric at plausible human eye height. A softly blurred embroidered edge of pale ivory silk occupies much of the left foreground, with heavy tassels, damp fibers and tiny beads of cloud moisture catching fragments of light. The banner moves slightly in the high wind and opens an irregular view toward the distance; it behaves as a natural curtain rather than a neat decorative frame.
>
> Beyond it, a monumental Chinese celestial gate rises at the end of vast white-jade stairs above an open sea of clouds. The gate is a threshold to an immense heavenly empire: layered flying eaves, Tang- and Song-inspired rooflines, deep dougong brackets, carved stone bases, restrained aged-gold roof details, flanking terraces and distant palace clusters receding into mist. Place the gate slightly off center, with one side opening toward a cloud-filled horizon and far palace roofs. Use a blank plaque or abstract cloud relief with no writing.
>
> Banks of cloud drift across the lower stairs and intermittently soften the gate base. A few tiny immortals in pale flowing robes ascend in small unhurried groups, occasionally hidden by the silk edge and low cloud. Metallic-white porcelain deity imagery remains secondary, appearing only as restrained crackle-glaze relief embedded in stonework and wall panels, with pale silver joints and fine muted-gold seams.
>
> Soft heavenly daylight diffused by high clouds is the primary source. Pale dawn light touches roof ridges, jade stair edges and selected silk threads while the concealed corridor remains gently shadowed. Deep atmospheric space, natural exposure, restrained film grain, matte jade and fabric textures, quiet reverence, observant rather than staged, no readable signage or decorative text.

## What this example demonstrates

- The camera is physically anchored near a foreground object.
- The banner reveals and conceals space irregularly instead of framing it symmetrically.
- The gate remains the hero while figures, clouds, relief, metal, and grain stay subordinate.
- Tang/Song identity comes from structural vocabulary rather than writing.
- Tiny unhurried figures provide scale and lived activity.
- One diffuse daylight system controls architecture, fabric, mist, and shadow.
- Material and atmospheric detail support depth without replacing composition.

## Known ambiguities to avoid copying

- `concealed corridor` introduces a corridor not previously established; name the actual camera-side space in new prompts.
- `metallic-white porcelain` should mean porcelain with a restrained cool sheen, not an undecided metal/ceramic material.
- `pale silver joints` should identify what is joined when used.
- Combine `soft heavenly daylight` and `pale dawn light` into one coherent cloud-diffused dawn source when both are intended.
