# Celestial Visual DNA

Use this reference when adapting the permanent visual DNA to a new scene or repairing an output that feels generic, staged, overdecorated, spatially implausible, or inconsistent.

## Constraint priority

Resolve competing instructions in this order:

1. The five permanent invariants: exact 21:9, silk banner, Tang/Song architecture, secondary porcelain relief, and no text.
2. The user's explicit scene, subject, action, and any additional locked variables.
3. Spatial and physical plausibility.
4. Clear subject hierarchy and narrative readability.
5. Lighting and material consistency.
6. Optional color, weather, grain, ornament, and surface detail.

Do not use a lower-priority preference to weaken a higher-priority requirement. When two items at the same priority directly conflict, expose the conflict instead of guessing.

## Silk banner

The banner is a recurring spatial signature, not a pasted-on motif.

Good signals:

- suspended from a beam, gate, terrace, corridor, bridge, pavilion, ceremonial pole, or other visible or strongly implied support;
- affected by wind, wake, movement, moisture, gravity, or contact;
- irregular edge, folds, tassels, translucency, embroidery, worn fibers, or selective highlights;
- creates depth, concealment, reveal, directional motion, or a transition between spaces;
- may sit in foreground, midground, or background according to the scene.

Failure signals:

- symmetrical curtains framing both sides like a theater stage;
- a floating strip of fabric with no support;
- a banner covering the main subject without narrative purpose;
- identical pale-ivory left-edge placement in every scene;
- excessive embroidery or text-like markings.

Variable properties include placement, number, color within the restrained palette, opacity, condition, wind response, and amount of occlusion. Keep its presence and physical motivation invariant.

## Tang/Song-inspired heavenly architecture

Use a unified Chinese architectural grammar rather than a random collection of decorative symbols. Choose only the structures the scene needs:

- layered flying eaves and readable roof ridges;
- deep dougong brackets and timber bay rhythm;
- beams, columns, corridors, thresholds, terraces, bridges, gates, stone bases, balustrades, or courtyards;
- pale stone or white-jade monumental elements;
- restrained aged-gold or muted-metal accents;
- layered palace clusters receding through cloud or atmospheric perspective.

Avoid generic fantasy castles, European arches, Gothic spires, theme-park symmetry, dense gilding, floating buildings with no structural logic, and ornament that substitutes for architecture.

Tang and Song references describe an idealized blended heavenly lineage. Do not claim strict historical reconstruction unless the user asks for one and accepts narrowing the visual language.

## Porcelain deity relief

Interpret `metallic-white porcelain` as white porcelain with a restrained cool metallic sheen, not as a fully metallic statue. Useful material cues include crackle glaze, pale silver joins, and fine muted-gold seams.

The relief must:

- remain visibly attached to an architectural surface;
- read as shallow or restrained sculptural relief;
- occupy less visual attention than the scene's main subject;
- share the light direction and atmospheric conditions of its carrier;
- avoid becoming a freestanding idol, giant face, luminous apparition, or central emblem.

When the scene is distant, the relief may appear as a small but identifiable inset material detail. Do not enlarge it merely to prove compliance.

## No-text treatment

Prefer architectural areas that do not invite lettering. When a plaque, tablet, banner surface, seal, wall panel, or ceremonial object would normally contain writing:

- leave it blank;
- use a clearly non-linguistic cloud, wave, or geometric relief;
- keep motifs organic or abstract rather than arranged like characters;
- explicitly exclude readable text, pseudo-characters, calligraphy, inscriptions, logos, and watermarks.

Do not solve the rule by blurring requested writing after the fact; do not request it at all.

## Scene adaptation

| Scene type | Banner placement | Architectural anchor | Relief carrier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monumental exterior | Near terrace edge, gate approach, bridge, or foreground support | Gate, palace, stairs, terrace, or cloud bridge | Gate base, balustrade, retaining wall, or terrace panel |
| Palace interior | Beam, side corridor, threshold, or column bay | Dougong, beams, columns, hall doors, ceiling, or stone plinth | Wall panel, screen base, column base, or altar surround |
| Cloud or aerial scene | Attached to terrace, sky vessel, bridge, pavilion, or distant gate | At least one terrace, bridge, pavilion, tower, or roofline | Parapet, vessel structure, bridge pier, or distant gate |
| Character close-up | Frame edge or background threshold without hiding the face | A readable fragment of eave, column, corridor, or balustrade | Small background wall, column base, or parapet inset |
| Ceremony | Asymmetrical sequence responding to wind and movement | Court, stair, gate, hall, terrace, or processional route | Stair wall, platform edge, gate pier, or court enclosure |
| Battle or pursuit | Pulled by wind, shock, movement, or damaged support | Bridges, roofs, terraces, gates, or corridors defining the action path | Background wall, bridge structure, or damaged architectural panel |
| Night scene | Silhouette, rim light, selective translucency, or reflected moonlight | Roof ridge, bracket, column, stone terrace, or gate silhouette | Small glazed highlight embedded in a lit architectural plane |

These are placement options, not fixed compositions. Select the minimum combination that makes the scene coherent.

## Cinematic restraint

- Anchor the camera at a plausible human, architectural, vehicle, or elevated-platform position.
- Prefer asymmetry, partial occlusion, negative space, and imperfect grouping when they support an observed moment.
- Create scale through small figures, architectural repetition, cloud layers, and receding silhouettes.
- Use one primary light source. Secondary bounce, reflection, or supernatural glow must remain explainable and subordinate.
- Apply gold, silver, glow, mist, and film grain selectively. Their purpose is separation or material definition, not generic spectacle.
- Keep jade, stone, silk, porcelain, timber, and metal materially distinct.
- Favor natural exposure and restrained contrast unless the scene specifically motivates stronger treatment.

## Targeted repair map

| Failure | Likely cause | Minimum repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banner looks like a picture frame | Symmetrical placement or no physical behavior | Move it to one motivated support; vary edge, fold, tassel, and occlusion |
| Banner floats | Missing attachment or gravity | Add beam, pole, gate, bridge, or terrace support and describe tension or sag |
| Scene feels like a game poster | Centered hero tableau, glossy surfaces, uncontrolled glow | Re-anchor the camera, add observed action, reduce gloss and glow, restore matte materials |
| Architecture feels generically Asian | Ornament named without structure | Add a small number of structural Tang/Song signals and remove unrelated fantasy forms |
| Architecture becomes a catalogue | Too many named components | Keep only elements that establish the current space and depth |
| Porcelain figure dominates | Excessive scale, contrast, glow, or central placement | Shrink it, embed it, lower contrast, and return attention to the narrative subject |
| Text or pseudo-text appears | Plaques and motifs invite lettering | Replace with blank surfaces or non-linguistic relief and restate the full no-text constraint |
| Scene is flat | Missing layer separation | Rebuild foreground, subject plane, and distant architecture with distinct scale and atmosphere |
| Main subject is obscured | Banner or cloud used without hierarchy | Change the occluder's edge or density while preserving its presence |
| Lighting is contradictory | Multiple unmotivated key lights | Choose one primary source and revise shadows, highlights, mist, and reflections together |
| Figures look staged | Symmetrical groups, front-facing poses, no interaction | Vary spacing and direction; add purposeful movement, gaze, contact, or partial concealment |
| Single-variable edit drifts | Preserve list omitted | Restate all locked elements and change only the named variable |
