# Behavioral Test Cases

These cases test decisions and observable behavior rather than exact wording.

## Case: Standard youth music festival brief

**Input**

A music festival targets young people interested in nightlife, experimental culture, and new experiences. The budget is fixed. The event needs four DJ camps, a transformed heavy truck used as a dance platform, and a short promotional runway. The user asks for three visual directions.

**Expected behavior**

- Separates the demographic description from a specific audience motivation.
- Distinguishes facts, interpretations, and creative leaps.
- Produces three directions that differ across core emotion, worldview, interaction mechanism, composition, material, and color-system logic.
- Applies the same budget and schedule constraints to all three directions.
- Explains which concepts are instantly understandable and which need audience education.
- Includes a pairwise differentiation matrix and a recommended test.

**Failure signals**

- Produces three “cyber” variants differentiated mainly by palette.
- Treats a famous festival's visual symbols as ready-to-use assets.
- Describes posters without explaining what participants do.
- Recommends a world that requires months of content without acknowledging the short runway.

## Case: Minimal input

**Input**

“给我做三个先锋音乐节的视觉方向。”

**Expected behavior**

- Recognizes that audience motivation, event format, schedule, and constraints are not known.
- Asks no more than three high-impact questions rather than issuing a long questionnaire.
- If the user cannot answer, states assumptions and confidence limits before proceeding.
- Does not invent current youth trends or audience research.

**Failure signals**

- Immediately produces polished directions based only on the word “先锋.”
- Asks for every possible Canvas field at once.
- Presents unsupported trend claims as facts.

## Case: Cultural reference without copying

**Input**

The user likes the communal intensity and impermanence of Burning Man and asks for a locally relevant event concept, but does not want a visual imitation.

**Expected behavior**

- Separates recognizable surface symbols from the gathering's participation engine.
- Explains what mechanism may transfer and why it fits the audience opportunity.
- Creates an original premise rather than defaulting to desert, flames, goggles, or post-apocalyptic styling.
- Flags localization, originality, and cultural-sensitivity questions.

**Failure signals**

- Produces “Chinese Burning Man” as the complete concept.
- Copies costumes, installations, palette, or naming while claiming localization.
- Mixes unrelated traditional symbols decoratively without an experience mechanism.

## Case: Strong idea, insufficient runway

**Input**

A “mystical night market” direction involves fortune-telling, relationship and wealth readings, strange stalls, NPCs, hidden rewards, and a large fictional world. Promotion begins in two weeks and the operations team is small.

**Expected behavior**

- Identifies worldbuilding, content, NPC, education, and operational dependencies.
- Does not reject the creative territory merely for being ambitious.
- Extracts a smallest testable attraction or marks the full version as future-stage.
- Compares the reduced concept fairly with directions under the same current constraints.

**Failure signals**

- Calls the full concept feasible because the visuals are compelling.
- Removes only decorative details while leaving the same operational load.
- Hides the amount of audience education required.

## Case: Structural duplication disguised as variety

**Input**

Three proposed directions share a futuristic world, euphoric mood, collect-and-unlock interaction, centered hero composition, glossy acrylic material, and high-contrast neon lighting. Their palettes are blue, red, and purple.

**Expected behavior**

- Rejects the set as one direction with three cosmetic variants.
- Locates the duplicate at the direction-skeleton level rather than the palette level.
- Rebuilds cultural territory, audience promise, worldview, or interaction before styling again.
- Reruns all six differentiation checks after repair.

**Failure signals**

- Passes the set because the hue names differ.
- Repairs it by renaming the directions or swapping moodboard images.
- Changes only typography or color.

## Case: Current-culture claim cannot be verified

**Input**

The brief claims that a particular youth subculture is currently surging, but provides no source and current research tools are unavailable.

**Expected behavior**

- Labels the claim as an unverified hypothesis.
- Separates the interpretation and creative leap from the missing observation.
- Suggests a proportionate check with representative participants or super players.
- Continues only with explicit assumptions when useful.

**Failure signals**

- Invents social-media posts, exhibition examples, interview results, or dates.
- Treats the client's claim as validated audience behavior.

## Case: Modify one existing direction

**Input**

The user asks to make Direction 2 warmer and less alienating without turning it into a new direction.

**Expected behavior**

- Preserves the direction's core emotion, worldview, interaction mechanism, composition, and material logic unless a necessary conflict is explained.
- Adjusts the requested color-system variable as a system, not just a hue swap.
- Checks that Direction 2 remains distinct from Directions 1 and 3.

**Failure signals**

- Rewrites the worldview or interaction without permission.
- Makes the palette warmer but leaves contrast, hierarchy, luminosity, and material response unchanged.
- Causes Direction 2 to collapse into another direction without detecting it.

## Case: Pure corporate identity brief

**Input**

A mature B2B company asks for a five-year corporate identity system with no live event or experiential component.

**Expected behavior**

- States that V0.1 is validated for live-event direction strategy, not long-term corporate identity.
- Does not pretend that participation mechanics are necessarily the right framework.
- Offers adaptation only if the user explicitly wants to experiment with the method.

**Failure signals**

- Forces NPCs, rituals, or live interactions into an unrelated identity brief.
- Claims the skill is already validated for all brand problems.
